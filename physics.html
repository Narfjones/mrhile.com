High School Physics Curriculum Map

STEELS Aligned - One Semester - 84 Minute Sessions

Semester Timeline

Explore the units of study for this physics course. Each section details the topics, key concepts, activities, and assessments, designed to provide a comprehensive and engaging learning experience. Click on any unit to expand its details.

Topics:

  • What is Physics? Branches of Physics.
  • The Scientific Method and Experimental Design.
  • Measurement, Significant Figures, and Scientific Notation.
  • Units and Dimensional Analysis (SI system).
  • Graphing Techniques and Data Interpretation.
  • Algebra and Trigonometry Review for Physics.

Key Concepts:

  • Observation, Hypothesis, Experimentation, Data Analysis, Precision, Accuracy, Variables.

Activities:

  • Lab: Designing and conducting a simple experiment (e.g., pendulum period).
  • Graphing exercises (linear, non-linear relationships).
  • Problem-solving: Unit conversions and basic algebraic manipulations.

Assessment:

  • Quiz on scientific method and units, lab report.

Topics:

  • Position, Distance, Displacement.
  • Speed, Velocity (average and instantaneous).
  • Acceleration.
  • Kinematic Equations for Constant Acceleration.
  • Free Fall.
  • Graphical Analysis of Motion (position-time, velocity-time, acceleration-time graphs).

Key Concepts:

  • Motion, Rate of Change, Vector vs. Scalar.

Activities:

  • Lab: Measuring and graphing motion (e.g., cart on a track, falling objects).
  • Problem-solving sessions applying kinematic equations.
  • Interpreting and sketching motion graphs.

Assessment:

  • Problem sets, lab report, quiz.

Topics:

  • Vectors and Scalars (review and expansion).
  • Vector Addition and Subtraction (graphical and component methods).
  • Projectile Motion (horizontal launch, angled launch).
  • Relative Motion (basic concepts).

Key Concepts:

  • Independence of Perpendicular Motion, Trajectory.

Activities:

  • Lab: Projectile motion investigation (e.g., launching projectiles, analyzing range and height).
  • Vector addition practice.
  • Problem-solving: Complex projectile motion scenarios.

Assessment:

  • Problem sets, lab report, quiz.

Topics:

  • Introduction to Forces (contact vs. field forces).
  • Newton's First Law (Inertia).
  • Newton's Second Law ($F = ma$).
  • Newton's Third Law (Action-Reaction Pairs).
  • Types of Forces: Weight, Normal Force, Tension, Friction (static and kinetic).
  • Free-Body Diagrams.
  • Applications of Newton's Laws (inclined planes, multiple objects).

Key Concepts:

  • Interaction, Equilibrium, Net Force, Acceleration, Mass.

Activities:

  • Lab: Investigating Newton's Laws (e.g., force and acceleration, friction coefficients).
  • Drawing and analyzing free-body diagrams.
  • Problem-solving: Applying Newton's Laws to various scenarios.

Assessment:

  • Problem sets, lab report, quiz.

Topics:

  • Application of Kinematics, Forces, Work & Energy, and Momentum concepts.
  • Scientific and Engineering Practices: Planning investigations, analyzing data, constructing explanations.

Key Concepts:

  • Synthesis of learned concepts, problem-solving through design.

Activities:

  • Students will work individually or in small groups to design, build, and test a device or model that demonstrates a specific physics principle from Units 1-4.
  • Examples: A device demonstrating projectile motion, a system illustrating conservation of energy, or a collision experiment.
  • Project proposal, design, construction, testing, and presentation.

Assessment:

  • Project functionality, design process, presentation, and explanation of physics principles.

Topics:

  • Work done by a constant force.
  • Kinetic Energy.
  • Work-Energy Theorem.
  • Potential Energy (Gravitational Potential Energy, Elastic Potential Energy).
  • Conservation of Mechanical Energy.
  • Power.
  • Introduction to simple machines (brief).

Key Concepts:

  • Energy Transformation, Conservation, Efficiency.

Activities:

  • Lab: Investigating conservation of energy (e.g., roller coaster simulations, spring energy).
  • Calculating work, energy, and power in different systems.
  • Analyzing energy transformations in real-world examples.

Assessment:

  • Problem sets, lab report, quiz.

Topics:

  • Momentum.
  • Impulse and Impulse-Momentum Theorem.
  • Conservation of Momentum.
  • Types of Collisions: Elastic and Inelastic Collisions.
  • Applications of Momentum (e.g., rockets, recoil).

Key Concepts:

  • Conservation of Momentum, Force over Time.

Activities:

  • Lab: Investigating momentum conservation in collisions (e.g., carts on a track).
  • Problem-solving: Analyzing various collision scenarios.
  • Designing experiments to test impulse.

Assessment:

  • Problem sets, lab report, quiz.

Topics:

  • Introduction to Waves (transverse vs. longitudinal).
  • Properties of Waves: Wavelength, Frequency, Amplitude, Speed.
  • Sound as a Longitudinal Wave.
  • Speed of Sound.
  • Intensity and Loudness.
  • Resonance and Standing Waves (brief).
  • The Doppler Effect (brief).

Key Concepts:

  • Wave Propagation, Energy Transfer, Vibration.

Activities:

  • Lab: Measuring the speed of sound or investigating resonance.
  • Exploring wave properties using simulations.
  • Listening activities to identify pitch and loudness.

Assessment:

  • Short problem set, mini-demonstration/explanation.

Topics:

  • Nature of Light (wave-particle duality, brief).
  • Reflection: Law of Reflection, Plane Mirrors.
  • Refraction: Snell's Law, Lenses (converging/diverging - basic ray tracing).
  • Total Internal Reflection (brief).

Key Concepts:

  • Light Interaction with Matter, Image Formation.

Activities:

  • Lab: Investigating reflection with mirrors and refraction with lenses.
  • Ray tracing diagrams for simple optical systems.
  • Observing optical phenomena.

Assessment:

  • Diagramming exercises, short quiz.

Topics:

  • Application of all course concepts.
  • Advanced Scientific and Engineering Practices: Refining designs, communicating complex ideas.

Key Concepts:

  • Comprehensive application of physics principles, iterative design, problem-solving.

Activities:

  • Students will undertake a more complex design challenge or research project that integrates multiple physics concepts learned throughout the semester.
  • Examples: Designing a simple musical instrument based on wave principles, building a periscope or simple telescope, or creating a device that uses energy transformations.
  • Detailed project proposal, iterative design and testing, final presentation, and written report.

Assessment:

  • Project functionality, scientific rigor, engineering design process, presentation, and comprehensive written report.
